# Historical Fraud Indicator Patterns (SYNTHETIC)

These are soft signals drawn from past investigations. They never trigger an action on
their own; the Coverage agent should cite them as supporting evidence alongside a rule.

## FI-01 High-value portable property shortly after inception
Theft of jewelry, watches, or electronics within the first 30 days of a new homeowners
policy was present in a disproportionate share of confirmed fraud cases. Ask for proof of
ownership (receipts, appraisals, photos predating the policy).

## FI-02 Same-day theft report without police reference
Theft reported to the insurer on the same day as the loss but without a police report
number is a weak indicator. Request the police report reference.

## FI-03 Amount matches the limit exactly
A claimed amount exactly equal to the policy limit - rather than an itemised total - is a
recurring pattern in inflated claims.

## FI-04 Clustered claims on one policy
Three or more claims of any peril on one policy within 12 months correlates with elevated
fraud and with maintenance-related losses. Review the full claim history.

## FI-05 "Sudden" water loss with gradual-damage signs
Claims described as sudden (burst pipe) on a property with prior slow-leak history often
turn out to include long-term seepage. Request plumber's report identifying the failure.

## FI-06 Vehicle total loss near the limit early in the policy term
Auto total-loss claims valued within a few percent of the limit, within the first year of
cover, should be checked against valuation guides and pre-loss vehicle inspection photos.
